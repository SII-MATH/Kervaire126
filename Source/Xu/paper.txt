                                                  Abstract. Using a Toda bracket computation hθ4 , 2, σ2 i due to Daniel C.
                                                  Isaksen [12], we investigate the 45-stem more thoroughly. We prove that θ42 = 0
                                                  using a 4-fold Toda bracket. By [2], this implies that θ5 exists and there exists
                                                  a θ5 such that 2θ5 = 0. Based on θ42 = 0, we simplify significantly the 9-cell
                                                  complex construction in [1] to a 4-cell complex, which leads to another proof
                                                  that θ5 exists.

                                                                  1. Introduction and main results
                                             The Kervaire invariant problem is one of the most interesting problems that
                                          relates geometric topology and stable homotopy theory. One way of formulating it,
                                          due to Browder [5], is in terms of the classical Adams spectral sequence (ASS) at
                                          the prime 2:
                                                                                                n+1
                                                    For each n, the element h2n ∈ Ext2,2              −2
                                                                                                           survives in the ASS.

                                          If h2n survives, we denote the corresponding detecting elements in homotopy by
                                          θn ∈ π2n+1 −2 S 0 and we say that θn exists. The strong Kervaire invariant problem
                                          for n is the following.

                                                           θn exists, and there exists a θn such that 2θn = 0.

                                          It is well-known that the first three Kervaire invariant elements θ1 , θ2 and θ3 can be
                                          chosen to be η 2 , ν 2 and σ 2 . And they all have order 2. Mahowald and Tangora [17]
                                          showed that θ4 exists and 2θ4 = 0 by an ASS computation. In [1], Barratt, Jones
                                          and Mahowald showed that θ5 exists by constructing a 9-cell complex and using
                                          the Peterson-Stein formula. Recently, using equivariant homotopy technology, Hill,
                                          Hopkins and Ravenel [10] in their marvelous paper showed that θn does not exist
                                          for all n ≥ 7, which left the existence of θ6 as the only open case.

                                             In [2], Barratt, Jones and Mahowald gave the following inductive approach to
                                          the strong Kervaire invariant problem:
                                          Theorem 1.1. Suppose that there exists an element θn such that 2θn = 0 and
                                          θn2 = 0. Then there exists an element θn+1 with 2θn+1 = 0.
                                            In this paper, we prove the following:
                                          Theorem 1.2. θ42 = 0.
                                            Since θ4 is unique and 2θ4 = 0, we have the following corollary:

   For the case θ5 , Lin [16] shows that there exists a θ5 such that 2θ5 = 0 based on
a computation of the Toda bracket hθ4 , 2, σ 2 i. Based on the same Toda bracket but
a different computational result, Kochman [13] also shows that θ42 = 0 and hence
that there exists a θ5 such that 2θ5 = 0. Recently, Isaksen [12] computed this Toda
bracket using more straightforward arguments. His result contradicts the results of
both Lin and Kochman. For more details about where Lin and Kochman’s argu-

computation of hθ4 , 2, σ 2 i gives a more complicated answer than the earlier claims,
we must study several other Toda brackets to prove θ42 = 0.
