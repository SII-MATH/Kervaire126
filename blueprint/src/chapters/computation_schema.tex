\section{Finite Adams presentations and coordinate conditions}
\label{chap:computation-schema}
% Certificate support now lives in KIP126/LinProgram/Certificates and Tactic.
% The native comparison now uses an untruncated source and one global map.

Finite generator, relation, and basis tables describe graded algebras and
coordinate expressions. To obtain conclusions about Adams spectral sequences,
one must compare these algebras with the actual pages and prove the stated
product, differential, and cell-map conditions. This section specifies those
mathematical objects and conditions.

\subsection{Finite presentations of an existing Adams page}

% SOURCE: project interface design; this does not assert any real computed value.
\begin{definition}[Finite basis multiplication table]
  \label{def:adams-e2-basis-table}
  \lean{KIP126.AdamsE2.Table}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:classical-adams-e2-e3-shape-slice}
  A table specifies a finite set $D$ of bidegrees containing $(0,0)$,
  dimensions $n(p)$ for $p\in D$, coordinates for the unit, and coefficients
  $c_{p,q,i,j,k}\in\mathbb F_2$ for every product whose source degrees
  $p,q$ and target degree $p+q$ belong to $D$.
  A degree outside $D$ is unknown, not a zero-dimensional space.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Algebra presented by a multiplication table]
  \label{def:adams-e2-table-model}
  \lean{KIP126.AdamsE2.Table.Model,KIP126.AdamsE2.Table.piece}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:adams-e2-basis-table}
  Introduce a generator $X_{p,i}$ of degree $p$ for each recorded basis
  index.  Let $A_T$ be the polynomial algebra over $\mathbb F_2$ modulo
  the unit relation and all covered relations
  \[
    X_{p,i}X_{q,j}=\sum_k c_{p,q,i,j,k}X_{p+q,k}.
  \]
  No relation sets an uncovered product to zero.  The degree-$p$ subspace
  is the image of the weighted homogeneous polynomials of degree $p$.
  This is a model algebra, not a definition of the actual Adams page.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Presentation of the actual $E_2$ page]
  \label{def:adams-e2-table-presentation}
  \lean{KIP126.AdamsE2.PageAlgebra,KIP126.AdamsE2.Presentation,KIP126.AdamsE2.Input}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:adams-e2-table-model,def:classical-adams-e2-e3-shape-slice}
  Fix an existing mod--$2$ Adams-shaped spectral sequence $E$ and a
  degree-preserving commutative algebra structure on the direct sum of its
  actual $E_2$ components.  A presentation consists of an algebra map
  $A_T\to E_2$, compatible maps on every homogeneous component, and
  bijectivity on each degree in $D$.  It also supplies bases of the actual
  page components, of the recorded sizes, equal to the images of the
  corresponding model generators.  The existence of such a presentation is a mathematical
  comparison condition; the numerical table alone does not establish it.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Constructing the fixed native sphere presentation}
\label{sec:lin-native-presentation-construction}

The following nodes refine the construction of $P$ in
Definition~\ref{def:computation-fixed-bindings}. They are open proof
obligations, not additional inputs to Challenge2. In particular, recovering
all native representatives is still to be established. The data algebra,
truncation compatibility and global presentation contract are implemented
separately; they do not close these actual comparison obligations. All comparisons use the existing
literature bindings, Milnor cooperations, and fixed sphere tower; the
construction must not read $P$ back from the delivery it is meant to produce.

\begin{proposition}[Correctness of the native resolution]
  \label{prop:lin-native-resolution-correct}
  \uses{def:lin-native-representative-specification}
  \notready

  maps, satisfy $\partial^2=0$ and the augmentation equation, and give an
  augmented projective resolution of $k$ in every degree needed below.
  In particular, at each required term the kernel equals the image of the
  preceding incoming differential, including exactness at the augmentation.
  Consequently $H^{s,t}(C)$ computes
  $\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal A}^s(k,k[t])$ for every $s,t\in\mathbb N$
  with $t\le261$.

  Certify projectivity from the specified free terms and exactness by
  checked algebraic witnesses. A finite computation must include the
  incoming and outgoing terms needed to compute each claimed cohomology
  group; an archive cutoff cannot be treated as a zero differential.
  If only a bounded fragment is recovered, prove a comparison with a
  genuine resolution, with explicit degree coverage. All $s\in\mathbb N$
  remain in the target: degrees outside the finite records need a proved
  vanishing or coverage argument. Square-zero alone is insufficient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Certified expansion retains the full native composition]

\begin{lemma}[Augmentation of the original native coefficient semantics]
  \label{lem:lin-secondary-augmentation}
  \lean{KIP126.Computation.Secondary.coproduct_unit,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.product_augmentation,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.augmentationTerms,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.augmentationTerms_coefficient,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.compose_augmentation,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.augmentation_eq_of_terms}
  \uses{def:lin-secondary-native-composition,lem:lin-secondary-certified-expansion}
  \leanok
  For every original rank, taking the coefficient of the all-zero Milnor
  monomial is multiplicative for the original all-monomial product predicate.
  For a module expression, filtering its unit terms and retaining their
  generator indices gives exactly this augmented coefficient at every target;
  repeated indices retain their parity. Augmentation of the original partial
  composition is the parity sum of paths whose two factors are units.
  Every original path must still resolve before this sum is formed, even
  when a missing nonunit term would have zero augmentation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The original coproduct sends the unit to its single pair of unit factors.
  Evaluation proves multiplicativity. Commuting the two finite filters
  identifies module coefficients, and induction on the complete resolved
  path list proves compatibility with composition. Thus an independently
  checked equality with a recorded generator list identifies its augmented
  coefficients. This does not prove a secondary associator formula,
  exactness or minimality of a resolution, or the actual sphere differential.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Native contraction is dual to the original monomial multiplication]
  \label{lem:lin-secondary-contraction}
  \lean{KIP126.Computation.Secondary.contractMonomial_eq_some,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.contractMonomial_eq_none_iff,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.contractPolynomial_coefficient,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.contractPolynomial_twice_coefficient,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.contractPolynomial_xi_zero_coefficient,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.contractModuleExpression_coefficient,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.contractMonomial_weight,KIP126.Computation.Secondary.outerContraction_eq_none_of_weight_lt}
  \uses{def:lin-secondary-native-composition,lem:lin-secondary-certified-expansion}
  \leanok
  At every original rank, coordinate subtraction by a dual monomial $a$
  succeeds with output $b$ exactly when the original monomial product
  $ab$ is the input; a rank mismatch or impossible subtraction is rejected.
  Its linear extension satisfies
  \[
    [b]C(a,p)=[ab]p
  \]
  at every monomial $b$ of that rank. The same identity holds at every
  target generator of a module expression. Two contractions compose by
  multiplying their dual factors, and contraction by $\xi_0^e=1$ is the
  identity. Successful contraction removes exactly the factor's weight.
  In the native second associator sum, its first factor
  $\xi_{m-k}^{2^k}$ has weight at least $2^{m-1}$ for $k<m\le r$;
  a smaller-weight input therefore contributes zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Induction on the original coordinate lists proves the subtraction
  characterization. Parity of the filtered complete polynomial or module
  expression gives the coefficient identities, including cancellation of
  repeated terms. Additivity of the original Milnor weight proves the
  degree obstruction and the stated exponential lower bound. These are
  components of the independent associator formula; the mod-four lift,
  division by two and complete formula still require separate proofs.
\end{proof}
