\section{External literature results}
\label{chap:external-results}

The published results below are grouped by their mathematical subject.
Their hypotheses, chosen classes and source locators are retained. Applying a
published theorem to the specified Adams towers or synthetic category requires
the stated comparisons; these applications remain unfinished wherever indicated.

\subsection{Notation and common mathematical choices}
\label{sec:literature-notation}

Use the completed sphere $S^0$, the mod--2 Eilenberg--Mac Lane spectrum $H$,
and the Milnor cooperations $M$ specified for these Adams constructions. Let $D$
denote the specified stable and synthetic categories together with their
synthetic analogue $\nu$, realization and quotient comparisons.
Write $E_r^{s,t}(X)$ for the actual classical Adams page and
$E_r^{s,t,w}(A)$ for its synthetic analogue. The classical
$F^s\pi_n(X)$ is the image of stage $s$ of the Adams tower.
Standard $h_j$ and their cup products use the same $H,M$ comparisons;
$\pi_n(X)$ and $\pi_{m,w}(A)$ are stable and synthetic mapping groups.

Write $S$ for the chosen classical sphere classes together with their
convergence identification, and $T$ for the chosen connective tmf spectrum,
its classes and its unit. In the formulas below, $S.\theta_5$, $S.\eta$,
$S.\nu$ and $T.\mathrm{spectrum}$ denote these mathematical choices.
Fix the synthetic class $\eta\in\pi_{1,2}S^{0,0}$ and the specified
comparisons with its classical counterpart, and choose the synthetic class
$h_0^{\mathrm{syn}}\in\pi_{0,1}S^{0,0}$ used in the low-degree results.
Each later theorem uses these
same choices. Their existence and the necessary model comparisons are not
established by this notation.

\begin{theorem}[Classical Hopf $d_2$ differential]
  \label{thm:external-adams-one-line}
  \uses{def:classical-adams-ss,def:adams-hj}
  \lean{KIP126.Challenge2.LiteratureResults.adamsOneLine_d2,KIP126.Interface.Solution.adamsOneLine_d2}
  \notready
  On the fixed sphere Adams tower, for every $j\in\mathbb N$ with $j\ge4$,
  \[
    d_2(h_j)=h_0h_{j-1}^2\ne0,
    \qquad (1,2^j)\longrightarrow(3,1+2^j).
  \]
  The $j=6$ instance is used in the main proof, and $j=4,5$ occur in the
  body's tool examples. May's 1964 thesis Part~II, Theorem~7.11 (PDF p.~158),
  and Ravenel's Theorem~1.2.12 give the recorded classical formula; the precise
  original Adams locator remains a provenance obligation in the manifest.
  The classes and their cup products are interpreted through the fixed
  internal comparison. Identification with the source calculation and the
  Solution proof remain unfinished.
\end{theorem}

% SOURCE: Ravenel, Complex Cobordism and Stable Homotopy Groups of Spheres,

\begin{theorem}[Positive-stem sphere Adams vanishing line]
  \label{thm:external-sphere-vanishing}
  \lean{KIP126.Classical.Adams.SphereVanishingLine,KIP126.Challenge2.LiteratureResults.sphereVanishing}
  \uses{def:classical-adams-ss,def:adams-hj}
  \notready
  For the actual mod--2 Adams tower of the fixed completed sphere,
  for every $s,t\in\mathbb Z$, its second page is zero in bidegree $(s,t)$ whenever

  this conservative specialization of Ravenel's Theorem 3.4.5(a).
  Identifying the source's spectral sequence with the fixed internal tower
  remains a model-comparison obligation. Finite CSV data cannot prove this
  all-degree vanishing statement; separation of the actual Adams filtration
  is a further internal application with convergence hypotheses.
\end{theorem}

% SOURCE: Burklund--Xu Proposition 7.19 AND its proof, at one original
% distinguished source choice; Source/BurklundXu/paper.txt:3475--3492.
\begin{theorem}[Original BX criteria at one distinguished synthetic choice]
  \label{thm:literature-route-bx}
  \lean{KIP126.Challenge2.LiteratureResults.route_bx}
  \uses{def:synthetic-theta5-choice,def:synthetic-lambda-quotient,def:adams-hj}
  \notready
  Put $Q=D.\mathrm{sphereFirstQuotient}$, keep the fixed
  $\eta\in\pi_{1,2}(S^{0,0})$, and let $q_r$ be the actual quotient-class
  map to $S^{0,0}/\lambda^r$. There exists a single
  $\theta\in\pi_{62,64}(S^{0,0})$ such that all three conditions below
  hold for that same $\theta$ and $\eta$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The first-quotient comparisons and order condition are
      \[
        Q^{1,2}(q_1\eta)=h_1,\qquad
        Q^{2,64}(q_1\theta)=h_5^2,\qquad \theta+\theta=0,
      \]
      and for every $r\in\mathbb N$ with $r\ge1$,
      \[
        \operatorname{SurvivesTo}
           \bigl(E(S^0),r+3,(2,128),h_6^2\bigr)
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_r(\eta\theta^2)=0.
      \]
      Survival to $E_{r+3}$ means the existence of a nonzero
      continuation of the specified $E_2$ class on that page.
    \item The actual first cofiber boundary satisfies
      \[
        \delta_1\bigl((Q^{2,128})^{-1}(h_6^2)\bigr)
          =\lambda\eta\theta^2
          \quad\text{in }\pi_{125,129}(S^{0,0}).
      \]
    \item The untruncated criterion is
      \[
        \operatorname{NonzeroSurvival}
          \bigl(E(S^0),(2,128),h_6^2\bigr)
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        \lambda\eta\theta^2=0
          \quad\text{in }\pi_{125,129}(S^{0,0}).
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
  The product $\eta\theta^2$ has bidegree $(125,130)$ before applying
  $\lambda$. This is the original distinguished-choice input of
  Burklund--Xu, Proposition~7.19 and its proof; source-to-model transport
  remains unproved. The finite formula is not replaced by the paper's
  later $\lambda\eta\theta^2$ modulo $\lambda^{r+1}$ normalization,
  and no quantifier over all other $\theta$ choices is added.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Massey products and Moss convergence}

% SOURCE: Moss (1970), Theorem 1.2; original scan NOT independently read.
% Cross-check only: Belmont--Kong arXiv:2112.08689v1, Theorem 1.1 p.2 /
% Theorem 4.11 p.12; IWX Theorem 2.16, background source:556--573.
\begin{theorem}[Moss convergence on the fixed sphere mapping model]
  \label{thm:literature-moss}
  \lean{KIP126.Challenge2.LiteratureResults.moss}
  \uses{def:classical-adams-ss,def:massey-product,def:toda-bracket,def:adams-detection}
  \notready
  Use the specified sphere mapping Adams towers, their composition
  pairing $P$ and their convergence identifications $c$.
  The object family is $\mathrm{Unit}\to C$, constant at the fixed
  $S^0$; it does not range over arbitrary spectra. Write
  $\mathcal E$ for the actual \emph{mapping} Adams sequence of
  $(S^0,S^0)$, and $\mathcal A_n$ for its fixed mapping abutment in
  stem $n$. Identification with $E(S^0)$ is a separate internal comparison.

  Assume $P$ is coherent. For all actual mapping objects, every
  $m\in\mathbb Z$ with $m\ge2$, and all applicable bidegrees and
  page elements, it satisfies the stem-signed Leibniz rule
  \[
    d_m(ab)=(d_ma)b+(-1)^{\operatorname{stem}(a)}a(d_mb),
  \]
  is associative, and takes two actual successor-page relations to
  the successor-page relation for their product.
  Also assume detection compatibility for every triple of indices
  in the constant family: for every $m\ge2$, every $u,v\in\mathbb Z^2$
  and all elements $a\in\mathcal E_m^u,b\in\mathcal E_m^v$ detecting
  specified $\alpha\in\mathcal A_{\operatorname{stem}(u)}$ and
  $\beta\in\mathcal A_{\operatorname{stem}(v)}$ using $c$, their product
  detects the actual shifted composite of $\alpha,\beta$, using the same $c$.

  Then for every $r\in\mathbb Z$ with $r\ge3$, every
  $i,j,k\in\mathbb Z^2$, and all
  \[
    a\in\mathcal E_r^i,\quad b\in\mathcal E_r^j,\quad
    c_0\in\mathcal E_r^k,\qquad
    \alpha\in\mathcal A_{\operatorname{stem}(i)},\
    \beta\in\mathcal A_{\operatorname{stem}(j)},\
    \gamma\in\mathcal A_{\operatorname{stem}(k)},
  \]
  suppose the following premises hold:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Both product mapping towers satisfy residual injectivity.
      Writing $A_s$ for their actual stages and $p_{s,t}:A_t\to A_s$
      for their restriction maps, this means on each tower
      \[
        \begin{aligned}
        \forall s\in\mathbb N,\ n\in\mathbb Z,\
                 u\in\pi_n(A_{s+1}),\quad
        &\bigl(\forall t\in\mathbb N,\ t\ge s+1
           \Longrightarrow \exists v\in\pi_n(A_t),\
                  (p_{s+1,t})_*v=u\bigr)\\
        &{}\land (p_{s,s+1})_*u=0
          \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u=0.
        \end{aligned}
      \]
    \item The three detections are
      $\operatorname{Detects}_{c}^{r,i}(a,\alpha)$,
      $\operatorname{Detects}_{c}^{r,j}(b,\beta)$ and
      $\operatorname{Detects}_{c}^{r,k}(c_0,\gamma)$.
      Each uses a common $Z_\infty$ representative and the actual
      tower-image associated graded under the selected $c$.
    \item The page products are zero:
      $P_r(a,b)=0$ and $P_r(b,c_0)=0$.
      For the actual shifted stable maps $f,g,h$ associated to
      $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ by the fixed closed adjunction,
      $f\mathbin{\gg}g=0$ and $g\mathbin{\gg}h=0$.
    \item There is no Moss-direction crossing for either product degree
      $\ell=i+j$ or $\ell=j+k$. For each of these $\ell$, there do not
      exist $m,q\in\mathbb Z$ satisfying
      \[
        r<m,\quad 0\le q<\ell_1-(r-1),\quad
        \ell_1<q+m,\quad
        d_m^{\,q,\ q+\ell_2-\ell_1+1}\ne0.
      \]
      These differ from the paper's extension-crossing conditions.
  \end{enumerate}
  Put $p=i+j+k-(r-1,r-2)$. There exist
  $x\in\mathcal E_r^p$ and
  $\xi\in\mathcal A_{\operatorname{stem}(p)}$ such that
  \[
    x\in\langle a,b,c_0\rangle_{\mathcal E_r},\qquad
    x\text{ is a permanent cycle},\qquad
    \operatorname{Detects}_{c}^{r,p}(x,\xi),\qquad
    \operatorname{Toda.Relation}
      \bigl(\operatorname{todaMap}(\xi),f,g,h\bigr).
  \]
  The shifted maps here are precisely
  $f=\operatorname{shiftMap}(S^0,S^0,i,
       \operatorname{stem}(j)+\operatorname{stem}(k),\alpha)$,
  $g=\operatorname{shiftMap}(S^0,S^0,j,\operatorname{stem}(k),\beta)$
  and $h=\operatorname{abutmentMap}(S^0,S^0,k,\gamma)$.
  The final Toda relation uses the fixed shift reindexing in
  $\operatorname{todaMap}(r,S^0,S^0,i,j,k,\xi)$.

  The Massey relation quantifies over representatives
  $a',b',c'$ on $\mathcal E_{r-1}$, their actual successor-page
  relations to $a,b,c_0$, and both defining-system choices $y,z$, with
  \[
    d_{r-1}y=P_{r-1}(a',b'),\qquad
    d_{r-1}z=P_{r-1}(b',c').
  \]
  The degrees of $y,z$ are respectively $i+j-(r-1,r-2)$ and
  $j+k-(r-1,r-2)$, and $x$ is the actual successor-page image of
  $P_{r-1}(y,c')-(-1)^{\operatorname{stem}(i)}P_{r-1}(a',z)$.
  Permanent cycle means the existence of a common $Z_\infty$
  representative, without requiring a nonzero $E_\infty$ image.
  No zero-indeterminacy hypothesis or survival of every Massey element
  is asserted.

  The canonical source is Moss, Theorem~1.2; its 1970 text has not been
  independently obtained or read. The directly read cross-check is
  Belmont--Kong v1, Theorem~1.1 (p.~2) and Theorem~4.11 (p.~12), with
  its multiplicative-setting restriction, together with IWX's
  Theorem~2.16. Matching all historical hypotheses and the chosen
  fixed mapping model remains an unfinished comparison.
  Its local $E_3$ specialization for $\langle h_5^2,h_0,B\rangle$
  remains unfinished; see Theorem~\ref{thm:main-local-moss-specialization}.
\end{theorem}
